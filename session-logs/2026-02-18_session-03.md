# Session 03 — 2026-02-18 ~18:20–18:45 EST

## What we set out to do

Build a virtual painter: draw with your finger in the air, and the projector renders the lines onto whatever surface you're pointing at. No physical markers needed. Connected the new Optoma 1080P projector (replacing the AAXA P7+) and the C922 camera.

## What actually happened

We built it, and it technically works — but the experience is bad enough that we're changing direction entirely.

### The build

Wrote app.py v0.5 with a full virtual painter:
- Projected toolbar at the top of the canvas: Draw | Erase | Claude | Clear
- Stroke buffer storing polylines, rendered as bright lines on the black pygame canvas
- Mode switching by pinching on toolbar buttons
- Started with pinch-and-drag for drawing, later pivoted to index-finger-only gesture

### The bugs (each one peeled back to reveal the next)

**1. Old calibration produced garbage coordinates.** Switching from the AAXA to the Optoma invalidated the saved homography. Cursor X values were like -4207, -8487 — miles off-screen. Deleted calibration.json, fell back to linear mapping. Lesson: calibration is projector-specific.

**2. Pinch flickered on/off every 1-2 frames.** Created dozens of 1-point strokes instead of continuous lines. Added debouncing (3 consecutive frames to confirm state change). Fixed — stroke #1 hit 208 continuous points.

**3. Pinch-to-draw is physically awkward.** Holding thumb + index together while moving precisely is hard. Switched to the standard virtual-painter approach: index finger only = draw, index + middle = move, pinch = toolbar actions.

**4. Finger-up detection was completely wrong.** Used `tip.y < PIP.y` (is the fingertip higher than the knuckle?). But the camera sees the hand from the side — the finger points horizontally at the wall, not vertically. Every finger read as "down" (`T____`). Rewrote using distance-based detection: finger is "up" if tip-to-MCP distance > PIP-to-MCP distance. Orientation-independent.

**5. Even with the rewrite, finger state was noisy.** Ring and pinky flickered constantly. Relaxed the check to only care about index + middle (ignore the rest). Added gesture debouncing too. Better, but still not reliable enough for smooth drawing.

### The pivot

After five rounds of bug-fixing, the core experience was still fighting us. The insight: **we were building an inferior pen.** A real pen on real paper has perfect precision, zero latency, tactile feedback, and costs nothing. The system should read physical work, not replace it.

**New direction:**
- Camera overhead, watching the desk — reads post-its, paper sketches, physical objects
- Screen in front of you — Claude's output goes here (real browser, no projector hacks)
- You interact with physical space naturally (draw, write, arrange things)
- Claude sees your workspace and builds on screen
- Trigger: voice, hotkey, or periodic auto-scan (TBD)
